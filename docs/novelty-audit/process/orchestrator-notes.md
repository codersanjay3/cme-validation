# Orchestrator notes

## During wave 1 (step 2)
- Pivot C looks weak on novelty: Gaspar-Massa-Matos (2005) churn rate is built from share changes valued at prices and explicitly nets out passive price changes; Yan-Zhang (2009, RFS 22(2), DOI 10.1093/revfin/hhl046, NOT hhm077 as given to B1) use it. So "value-weight turnover is contaminated by price drift" is the reason the standard measure exists. CME's finding is a correction of its own nonstandard measure, not a contribution. Verify with GMM full text (B1).
- Pivot D risk: horizon homophily in co-holding could be mechanical (quasi-indexers hold near-index portfolios and have low turnover; similar size/style). Needs controls for style/size/index-tracking. No paper found yet that states the fact directly (Georg-Pierret-Steffen "Similar Investors" is the nearest, MMF/banks).
- Pivot B prior art: Cella-Ellul-Giannetti (2013 RFS) short-horizon owners sell more in turmoil; JFM 2021 horizon x concentration; Sun 2015 heterogeneity of institutional ownership and price delay; Glossner et al COVID: institutions amplified, retail absorbed. Dispersion-of-horizons as a stock characteristic exists in at least one paper (search snippet: "dispersion of churn rates across holders scaled by average churn"; source not yet pinned). Novelty thin unless the within-stock absorption mechanism (who trades against whom) is new.
- Pivot A: peer effects in holdings/trades are well studied (Hong-Kubik-Stein 2005, Pool-Stoffman-Yonker 2015, Blocher 2016 flows/network externalities, Follow-the-Leader AQR). No paper found yet on peer effects in turnover/horizon. Parsa (MIT thesis) finds trading frequency is largely an investor fixed effect: a threat (little to transmit) and a hook (if it moves, why?). Most promising so far.
- Pivot E: LeBaron (2001) already has evolving horizons in an ABM; Li-Nishimura-Men (2014) FMH ABM. Horizon-similarity network formation may be new, but an ABM alone rarely lands in IF>4 finance journals.
- Journal IF check needed: FRL itself has JCR IF well above 4 (around 7 to 10 in recent years) — the user's threshold may already be met by the current target. Verify current IFs in wave 2.

## Wave 2 (B10, Pivot B)
- PRE-EMPTION: Chan, Cheng, Hameed (JFQA 57(7) 2022) "Investor Heterogeneity and Liquidity": STDCR = cross-holder sd of GMM churn / mean churn; higher dispersion -> more liquidity, lower liquidity volatility; 2003 dividend-tax reform shock; flow-induced trading has more price impact in low-heterogeneity stocks. So "horizon dispersion matters" is published at JFQA. Remaining room for B: volatility/crash/price-delay outcomes, stress-episode within-stock absorption by horizon (argued, not tested, in Chan et al.), contamination-free measure. B is now a secondary test, not a headline.
- Their sign agrees with the CME stock-level data (lower dispersion -> more fragile) and contradicts the CME simulated friction channel: the model's channel is the outlier.
- Other: Dai et al (FinRev 2022) short-horizon share + Russell design; Timmer (JFE 2018) insurers/pensions countercyclical (by type); Bolton-Santos-Scheinkman (QJE 2011) theory of long-run investors supplying outside liquidity.
