CURRENT PAPER. "Chronotypic Market Ecology: Temporal Homophily, Network Fragmentation, and Market Stability" (Subramanian and Seetharaman, 2026, prepared for Finance Research Letters). An agent-based model in which investors differ by lookback horizon tau; agents with similar tau preferentially link (temporal homophily, strength kappa_h); horizons adapt toward network neighbours' horizons (assumed adaptation rate 0.06). Simulation: more homophily lowers volatility 1.31x through a cross-horizon friction channel and lowers belief fragmentation 14x; adaptation compresses horizon diversity ("temporal echo chambers"). Framed as a network microfoundation for the Fractal Market Hypothesis (Peters 1994). Empirics on SEC 13F holdings of the top 150 filers, 2013Q2 to 2026Q1: (1) horizon tau = 63 / quarterly turnover; (2) institutions with similar horizons hold more similar portfolios (pairwise correlation of cosine portfolio similarity with horizon proximity, mean 0.158, positive in all 50 quarters, Newey-West t = 19); (3) an aggregate horizon-diversity index (Shannon entropy of log tau) correlated with VIX at r = -0.53, but this vanishes (r = -0.06) when turnover is computed from share changes instead of value weights, i.e. it was price drift; (4) stock level, 63,000 stock-quarters, survivors only: lower ownership-weighted dispersion of holders' log tau predicts slightly higher next-quarter volatility (within-stock slope -0.011, t = -2.4), the sign the Fractal Market Hypothesis suggests but opposite to the model's simulated friction channel; (5) bond funds from N-PORT, descriptive. Data available: 13F holdings panel, share-based and value-weight horizons per institution-quarter, daily prices for mapped survivors, N-PORT bond fund holdings.
