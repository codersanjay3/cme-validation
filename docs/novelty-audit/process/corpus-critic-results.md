# Corpus critic results (step 8)

## cc-4 (shock-design critique) — overturning evidence FOUND for merger shocks
- Lewellen-Lowry (JFE 2021): merger sample dominated by BlackRock-BGI 2009 (post-crisis timing, growth tilt); corrected, no effect. Massa-Schumacher-Wang (RFS 2021): mergers directly shift neighbours' holdings (anticipated fragility) -> exclusion restriction fails for horizon contagion. Gilje-Gormley-Levit: treatment intensity ambiguous.
- Better shock precedent: Liu et al. (RoF 2023) fund-manager parental deaths (n=304) push funds toward quasi-indexing (lower active share). Manager deaths/departures become the primary shock; mergers only pooled, ex-BGI, robustness.
- Effect on positions: identification investigator's "merger/closure event design" weakened; manager-shock arm strengthened. Confidence that A is identifiable at top-field standard: ~50% (was ~70% conditional on shock design).

## cc-8 (theory) — strengthening evidence FOUND
- Froot-Scharfstein-Stein (JF 1992), Chamley (TE 2007), Cespa-Vives (2011): strategic complementarity among short-horizon traders, multiple equilibria. Gives A a sign prediction (positive peer effect) and a nonlinearity/tipping prediction; horizon exogenous in all, so endogenous adaptation remains the novel part.

## MacDonald SSRN 5692342 (read via author site + SSRN listing, orchestrator)
- "Are Investors Influenced by the Trades of Other Investors? A Quantity-based Approach for U.S. Institutional Holdings" (Austin MacDonald, JHU Carey; JMP 2024-25; SSRN Nov 2025). 13F 1980-2023; investors binned by size and benchmark correlation; influence coefficient = % change in a stock held by the influenced investor after a 1% change in the same stock by the influencer. Finding: low overlap; passive investors influence active ones regardless of size.
- Verdict: NOT a pre-emption of Pivot A (outcome is stock-level quantity, the established holdings/trades estimand, not institution-level horizon/turnover). It is the closest recent 13F institutional-influence paper and should be cited as the nearest neighbour; its passive-to-active influence finding is a mechanism relevant to horizon contagion (low-turnover investors influencing high-turnover ones).
