## Draft A
Strongest thesis: the defensible pivot is 'horizon contagion' — the first identified peer effect in institutional investment horizon through portfolio-overlap networks, identified with manager-level shocks to neighbours on the full SEC 13F universe, grounded in strategic-complementarity theory (Froot-Scharfstein-Stein), with the CME model recast as a calibrated consequence model and holder-horizon dispersion as a consequence section. Argue it forcefully and specify the paper.

## Draft B
Steelman-contrarian: argue that horizon contagion is a trap — novel only because it is unidentifiable (common style-level turnover, sampled networks, merger-shock critiques, trait persistence) — and that the most defensible publishable pivot is instead a US stress-episode liquidity-absorption-by-horizon paper with a measurement-integrity contribution (13F unit break, share-based horizons), extending Chan-Cheng-Hameed and Cella-Ellul-Giannetti. Defend this minority view seriously.

## Draft C
Synthesis-reconciler and practitioner: argue both sides capture part of the truth, and lay out a staged, falsifiable research programme with explicit kill criteria and decision points: step zero data rebuild, a pre-registered variance/peer test, then the shock design, with B and E as modules that are publishable on their own if A fails; map each module to target journals by impact factor and fit.
